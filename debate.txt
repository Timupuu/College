Ethical hacking is not another form of cybercrime. 
It constitutes the authorized access and exploitation of a system while being bounded by laws and contracts. 
There are strict guidelines to exploit the system and continuous reporting to the employer regarding the exploit.
There is a rule of thumb to do as much as told and if there are new paths or exploits are found they are to be reported to the employer.

Points:
1. It is the exploitation or penetration bounded by straight through contract and agreements
2. It is more of like tinkering with a piece of software or program under the authorization/supervision of the owner
3. It is done to push the system to its limits and find out the possible vulnerabilities
4. Edward snowden doesn't fall under ethical hacking rather he is a whistle blower and its leaktivism/hacktivism (The case of snowden wasn't legal even though it was moral. Ethical hacking is legal and moral)
5. It is done by professionals with certifications from internationally recognized organizations like EC-Council (E commerce) CompTIA (computing technology industry association)
6. Major tech giants run bug bounty and hack-fest, they are also a epitome of the legality of ethical hacking. 
7. Ethical hacking is done to harden the system and prevent illegal hackers to exploit the system.
8. Same tools doesn't mean same purpose. A kitchen knife can cook food or harm someone it depends on the intent of the user. Intent matters
